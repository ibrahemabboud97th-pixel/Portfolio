Research profile

A tripartite profile

Clinical practice, academic research and science communication, held together by a commitment to humanitarian action.

I am a medical graduate of Latakia University (formerly Tishreen University) and an anesthesiology resident. Daily work in the operating room and the neuro-ICU gives me a firsthand view of how anesthetic agents modulate central nervous system function, and of how patients with stroke or TBI recover consciousness.

That clinical view drives my research: molecular strategies to cross the blood-brain barrier, computational models of neural circuits, and better neuromonitoring (such as EEG) in resource-limited hospitals.

Alongside medicine, I co-founded a charity for cancer patients. It taught me that healthcare and humanitarian commitment are deeply intertwined, and that what matters is what you achieve despite the constraints.

Humanitarian leadership

Ma'ak Association for Supporting Cancer Patients

From a student volunteer initiative in 2019 to a licensed NGO supporting cancer patients, orphans and disaster-affected families.

Co-founder & executive leader

After our volunteer team "We Care" had to stop, I co-founded Ma'ak to keep children's cancer care alive. I handled the legal process across several governorates until we received our license (Ministry of Social Affairs and Labor, No. 2789, 22 Nov 2022).

I built the core departments from scratch (Medical, HR, Media, Finance), authored the annual strategic plans, and represented the association in health-sector coordination meetings with the WHO, while staying on the ground in psychosocial sessions with children.
